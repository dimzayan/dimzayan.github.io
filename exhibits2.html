Do Right Hall occupies a converted space on the western edge of Marfa's quiet grid. At nearly five thousand feet above sea level, in the high Chihuahuan Desert, it holds a quality of light that is both ruthless and clarifying — horizontal, unwavering, without apology. It is a space that strips context away from work and asks it to exist on its own terms.

Marfa has been a locus for serious art since Donald Judd arrived in the early seventies, drawn by the same merciless clarity. The landscape does not yield to the work. It asks the work to reckon with something older and more patient than institutions. The paintings in this exhibition were made in a studio two blocks away, and carry that reckoning in their surface.

Artist
Dim Zayan
b. France, 1977
Based in Marfa, TX

Dim Zayan works in oil and epoxy on raw gypsum panels, using an angle grinder to carve and incise the surface before painting begins. The gypsum is not a neutral support — it is sedimentary, dense with its own history, and it resists. The grinder removes as much as it reveals, leaving behind channels and compressions that the paint then pools into, or is repelled by.

Trained in France and working for two decades across Europe and the Americas, Zayan arrived in Marfa drawn by the proximity of landscape to practice. The high desert of West Texas — its silence, its scale, its particular insistence — has become the primary subject of his work, not as representation but as physical pressure. The paintings are not images of land. They are records of time spent inside it.

Zayan co-founded MarfaMUST, an artist-for-artist collective that operates outside the institutional framework, presenting work in spaces chosen for their resistance to the expected.
