Do Right Hall sits on the western edge of Marfa's quiet grid — a converted structure that holds the particular quality of the high Chihuahuan Desert light. At nearly five thousand feet, the air is thin and the light is absolute. Horizontal, direct, without negotiation. It is a space that asks the work to stand without the insulation of conventional gallery conditions.

Marfa has drawn serious practitioners since Donald Judd arrived in the early seventies, drawn by the same quality of light and the distance from institutional frameworks. The landscape here does not recede into background. It remains present, pressing. The paintings in this exhibition were made two blocks away, in a studio facing west, and they carry that pressure in their surface and in their making.

Artist
Dim Zayan
b. France, 1977
Based Marfa, TX

Dim Zayan works in oil and epoxy on raw gypsum panels, using an angle grinder to carve and incise the surface before painting begins. The gypsum is not a neutral support — it is sedimentary, geological, resistant. The grinding removes as much as it reveals, leaving channels and compressions that the paint pools into or is repelled by. Surface and depth are inseparable.

Born in France in 1977 and working across Europe and the Americas, Zayan arrived in Marfa drawn by the proximity of landscape to practice. The high desert of West Texas — its silence, its scale, its particular insistence — has become the primary ground of his work, not as representation but as physical pressure. These paintings are not images of land. They are records of time spent inside it.

Zayan co-founded MarfaMUST, an artist-for-artist collective presenting work outside institutional frameworks, in spaces chosen for their resistance to the expected.
